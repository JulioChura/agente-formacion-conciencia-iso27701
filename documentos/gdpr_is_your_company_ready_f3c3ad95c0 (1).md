

# GDPR Is your company ready? 



#### General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) 

In an increasingly interconnected economy, data related to daily life, location and personal interests are becoming increasingly accessible. It happens because people often provide personal information when hiring a service or when making an online purchase, without any guarantee of what will be done with this data, for how long it will be saved or to whom it will be conveyed. Personal data often ends up circulating on the internet which is the result of countless cases of data leakage or misuse, which usually ends up being inconvenient and causing a lot of headaches for the data owner. 

In this context, the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) was created with the objective of increasing a companies’ responsibility for how they handle personal information as well as preventing abuse and the unauthorized use of data. It is important to remember that personal data does not belong only to customers, it also applies to the data of employees, service providers and shareholders, among others. 

Complying with this regulation means ensuring that all personal data managed by the company are collected legally. In this way, the owners of data are informed about how their data is treated and the organization ensures that they are kept safe, in other words, protected against breaches. 

#### Challenges 

Companies of different scales and segments are still struggling to adapt their activities and ensure compliance with the GDPR. This is because the regulation does not only affect the IT department. It must be implemented and followed by several other areas of companies such as human resources, marketing, finance, and any other department that deals with personal data. Therefore, the management of all this information becomes extensive and laborious. 

Moreover, there is a need to implement documents, policies, terms and change of contracts, which results in a very complex governance of personal data governance, especially when performed manually. 

The challenges are numerous and the legal and financial implications are extensive. Those who violate the GDPR privacy and security standards will suffer severe penalties. It is for this reason that it is necessary for all organizations to dedicate themselves to this data protection standard that the regulation imposes, making structural and mainly cultural adjustments while aiming to implement rules of conduct that apply to the entire company.

##### Management of Personal Data Breach 

The GDPR requires that in case of a personal data breach, the controller must notify the competent control authority of the fact. This notification must contain information such as: 

- Nature of the personal data breach including the categories and approximate number of 

- data subjects concerned; 

- Name and contact details of the data protection officer (DPO) or other contact point where more information can be obtained; 

- Description of the likely consequences of the personal data breach; 

- Description of the measures taken or proposed to be taken by the controller to address the personal data breach, including, where appropriate, measures to mitigate its possible adverse effects.
